Pet Age Calculator: The Complete Guide

July 21, 2026

How old is my dog "in human years"? The commonly cited "multiply by 7" rule isn't actually accurate -- dogs age much faster in their first year or two, and after that, aging speed depends on their size. PetAgeCalc is a free online tool that accurately converts dog and cat ages to human years based on the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) chart. All calculations happen entirely in your browser.

Key Features

🐶
Size-Based Conversion (Dogs)
Splits into 4 size categories -- small, medium, large, and giant -- to reflect their different aging rates.
🐱
A Dedicated Chart for Cats
Since cats don't vary much in size between breeds, a single conversion chart is used.
🔢
Decimal Age Input
Enter a decimal age like "3.5 years" to account for months as well as years.
🔒
Fully Local Processing
All calculations happen entirely in your browser and are never sent to a server.

How to Use It

1

Choose Dog or Cat

Select which type of pet you have. Choosing dog reveals a size selection field.

2

Choose a Size (Dogs Only)

Based on your dog's adult weight, select small (up to 20 lbs), medium (20-50 lbs), large (50-90 lbs), or giant (over 90 lbs).

3

Enter the Age and Calculate

Enter your pet's age in years (e.g. 3.5). Click the 🐾 Calculate button and the age converted to human years appears instantly.

⚠️ Note: This result is an average estimate based on the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) and International Cat Care (ICatCare) charts. Actual aging can vary by individual health and genetics, so always check with a veterinarian for an accurate assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is the Pet Age Calculator really free?

Yes, it is completely free to use with no sign-up or installation required. There is no limit on how many times you can calculate.

Q. Why do I need to enter a dog's size separately?

Because larger dogs age faster than smaller ones. According to the AVMA chart, small dogs gain 4 human years per year, while giant dogs gain 7.

Q. Does it calculate differently by cat breed?

No. Unlike dogs, cats don't vary much in size between breeds, so a single conversion chart is used.

Q. Does this result reflect my pet's exact health status?

No. The chart is a reference figure based on average aging rates -- actual aging can vary by individual health and genetics. Always check with a veterinarian for an accurate assessment.
