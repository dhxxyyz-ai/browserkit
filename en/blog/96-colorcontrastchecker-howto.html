Color Contrast Checker: The Complete Guide

July 24, 2026

You picked a button text color because it looked nice, only to find it barely stands out from the background on a bright screen? A combination that looks fine in a design mockup can be completely unreadable for people with low vision or anyone viewing the screen outdoors. ColorContrastChecker is a free online tool that instantly calculates the WCAG 2.1 contrast ratio from just a text color and a background color, and tells you whether it passes the AA/AAA grades. All calculations happen entirely in your browser.

Key Features

⚖️
WCAG Contrast Ratio Calculation
Calculates the relative luminance of two colors to give you the exact contrast ratio, from 1:1 to 21:1.
✅
Automatic AA/AAA Grading
Shows at a glance whether you pass all 4 WCAG grades for normal and large text.
👁️
Live Preview
See exactly how real text looks with your color combination, in both large and body text sizes.
🔒
Fully Local Processing
Your colors are calculated entirely in your browser and are never sent to a server.

How to Use It

1

Enter the Text and Background Colors

Type a HEX code directly, or use the color picker to choose the text color and background color.

2

Check the Contrast Ratio and Preview Automatically

The contrast ratio is calculated the moment you enter a value, and a preview of large and normal text with your actual color combination appears.

3

Check AA/AAA Pass/Fail

Whether you pass or fail all 4 grades for normal and large text is laid out in a table.

⚠️ Note: WCAG defines "large text" as 18pt (24px) or larger, or 14pt (about 19px) or larger and bold. Everything else falls under the "normal text" threshold.

How WCAG 2.1 Contrast Ratio Is Calculated

GradeMinimum Contrast
AA · Normal Text4.5 : 1 or higher
AAA · Normal Text7 : 1 or higher
AA · Large Text3 : 1 or higher
AAA · Large Text4.5 : 1 or higher
💡 A real calculation example: Gray text #767676 on a white background (#FFFFFF) → the contrast ratio is about 4.54:1, which barely passes the AA normal text threshold (4.5:1) but fails AAA (7:1). By contrast, pure black (#000000) against white (#FFFFFF) gives 21:1, the maximum, which passes every grade. This shows how even a slightly muted color can fail to meet accessibility standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What are the WCAG AA/AAA thresholds?

Under WCAG 2.1, normal text needs at least 4.5:1 for AA and 7:1 for AAA, while large text (18pt or larger, or 14pt bold) needs at least 3:1 for AA and 4.5:1 for AAA.

Q. Is the Color Contrast Checker really free?

Yes, it is completely free to use with no sign-up or installation required. There is no limit on how many times you can check.

Q. Is the color information I enter sent to a server?

No. All calculations happen entirely in your browser, and the colors you enter are never sent to or stored on an external server.

Q. Why does accessibility compliance matter?

Low-contrast text is hard to read not only for people with low vision or color blindness, but also for anyone viewing a screen outdoors. Government and corporate websites are also often required to comply with accessibility guidelines.
