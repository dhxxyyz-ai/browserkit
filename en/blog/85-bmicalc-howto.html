BMI Calculator: The Complete Guide

July 19, 2026

At 170cm and 65kg, is that a normal weight or overweight? The number on the scale alone doesn't tell you, but plug it into the BMI formula (weight ÷ height²) and the answer is immediate. BmiCalc calculates BMI and weight category the moment you enter your height and weight, and if you add your age, gender, and activity level, it also shows your basal metabolic rate (BMR) and total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. See the worked example below.

How to Use It

1

Enter Height and Weight

Enter your height (cm) and weight (kg). These two values alone are enough to calculate BMI.

2

Choose Whether to Calculate Metabolic Rate

If you only need BMI, choose "BMI only." If you also want your recommended daily calories, choose "Include metabolic rate" and enter your age, gender, and activity level.

3

Check the Results

Click the 📏 Calculate button and your BMI value and weight-category badge appear front and center; if you chose to include metabolic rate, you'll also see the BMR and TDEE breakdown below.

⚠️ Note: BMI is classified using the World Health Organization (WHO) standard, and metabolic rate uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Body composition (muscle mass, body fat percentage) isn't factored in, so use this as a reference only, and check with a doctor or professional for an accurate assessment of your health.
💡 Real example: At 170cm and 65kg → BMI = 65 ÷ 1.70² = 22.5, comfortably within the Normal range (18.5-24.9). At the same height, weight would need to reach about 73kg (BMI 25.3) to cross into Overweight — a difference of just a few kg can shift the category.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is the BMI Calculator really free?

Yes, it is completely free to use with no sign-up or installation required. There is no usage limit either.

Q. Is the height, weight, and age information I enter sent to a server?

No. All calculations happen entirely in your browser, and nothing you enter is ever sent to or stored on an external server.

Q. If my BMI is normal, does that mean I'm healthy?

BMI is a reference figure calculated only from height and weight, and doesn't account for muscle mass or body fat percentage. For example, someone with a lot of muscle mass can have a high BMI while still having a low body fat percentage. Always consult a doctor or professional for an accurate health assessment.

Q. What's the difference between basal metabolic rate (BMR) and total daily energy expenditure (TDEE)?

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the minimum energy your body burns at complete rest, while total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) adds your daily activity level to give the total calories you actually burn in a day. TDEE is typically used as the baseline for diet and meal planning.
